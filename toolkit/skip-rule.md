# The skip rule

Life gets in the way. Plan for it, so one missed day does not turn into giving up.

## The rule

**One skipped day is fine. Never skip twice.**

## What to do

1. **Notice it without judging.** Write one line about what got in the way.
2. **Do the 5-minute version the next day.** Shrink that day's card to its smallest piece. For example, on day 1, write only three lines of your list.
3. **Do not double up.** Pick up where you left off. James Clear notes that trying to make up for a miss by doing twice as much has never worked well for him.
4. **Allow slack.** The guide holds 15 days of work. Give yourself up to 21 calendar days to finish it.
5. **If you lose a 1-hour slot** (day 7 or day 15), make the 30-second version of the piece and publish it.

## Why this works

- **Lally et al.:** In a study of 96 people over 12 weeks, missing one opportunity to do the behavior did not materially affect habit formation. People who were very inconsistent, missing it often, did not form habits.
- **Never miss twice (James Clear):** The first miss is rarely the problem. The spiral of repeated misses is. His advice is to get back as soon as you can, and to reduce the scope to the smallest version you can do.
- **Google field experiment (Beshears et al.):** In a study of 2,508 Google employees, people paid for exercising only in a fixed time window were 10 percentage points less likely to visit the gym weekly once the payments stopped, compared with 5 points for those paid for exercising at any time. Flexibility held up better.
- **Emergency reserves (Sharif and Shu):** In a study of more than 270 students and staff with a step goal, people with a few permitted skip days reached their goals up to 40% more often. After a miss, 55% of those with reserve days met their goal the next day, compared with 37% of those with a strict seven-day target.

These studies are small and come from specific settings, such as exercise and step goals. Treat them as support for the rule, not as proof for content creation.
