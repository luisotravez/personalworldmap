# Choose your tools

Three small decisions. None of them is permanent. Pick what gives you the least resistance and change it later if you need to.

## 1. Where will you publish? (pick one primary place)

- **Where are the people you want to reach?** Treat this as an assumption. As a starting reference, look at where you have found the most content about the topic you want to talk about. Later, it can become data.
- **What format can you sustain?** If you have made something before, whether written, audio only or video, base your choice on the one that gives you the least resistance.
- **Where have you published before?** Again, choose by where you feel the least resistance.

Start with one primary place. You can repost elsewhere later.

## 2. Where will you keep your notes?

| Option | Good for | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Drive | The simplest start. Works on your phone and is easy to share by link | Few ways to link ideas to each other |
| Notion | Tracking your progress and organizing days in a database | Another app to learn |
| Obsidian | Linking ideas to each other. Notes are plain Markdown files on your device | The app is free for personal use. Cross-device sync is a paid add-on, and the project says there are free alternatives compatible with it, such as syncing the folder through a cloud drive |

The author's pick: **Google Drive**. Obsidian is a good upgrade if you want something more complete. Check Obsidian's own terms on commercial use if you work inside a company.

## 3. Do you want an AI to help? (optional)

The guide works without one. If you use one, the author's pick is **Claude**, and any assistant works.

Use the 4Ds from the AI Fluency Framework as a checklist:

- **Delegation:** decide what you do with AI and what you do yourself.
- **Description:** give it the context it needs.
- **Discernment:** check what it gives you critically.
- **Diligence:** take responsibility for what you publish, and be open about the role AI played.

You decide how much it writes for you. Say so openly if you want your audience to know.

## If you worry about being seen

- If you worry that someone you know might see it, you can always start an account from scratch.
- If it is needed, block the people who make you most anxious. You can unblock them later.
- If it feels even more intense but you still want to start, you can post to Close Friends so only a handful of people see it. Keep in mind this will not tell you what to improve. It is only an exercise for yourself.
