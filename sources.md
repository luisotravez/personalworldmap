## How to read this file

- **Status:** `confirmed` (chosen as a base source), `kept` (in unless feedback says otherwise), `deep dive` (to be explored in depth), `candidate` (needs review).
- **Role:** `foundational`, `recommended reading`, `evolution` (how the idea of personal brand is changing), `counterpoint` (declares a risk or a different view), `end game` (what a world can become), `practice` (a concrete method).
- **Layer and day:** proposed only. Layer names (Approach, Concepts, System, Tools, Environment) are provisional.
- Everything here is paraphrased. For paid products we only reference what is public.
- Spanish-language sources are marked `ES`. All others are in English.

## 7. Milestone 1: the introduction video (day 7)

The day 7 video is an "introduce yourself" video that can be posted on any platform. It covers who you are and what your audience can expect. It is not a YouTube channel trailer. Most sources below come from brand or marketing contexts and answer "who do I help and why follow". The guide will adapt them to a world frame: what your world is, the through line that connects your topics, and why it matters to you.

### Hollyland: Introduce Yourself Vlog Script Example
- **Link:** https://store.hollyland.com/blogs/creator-hub/introduce-yourself-vlog-script-example
- **Type:** Article with a sample script
- **Status:** candidate
- **Role:** practice
- **Layer / day:** Tools. Days 6 and 7 (proposed)
- **What it offers:** A three-part structure: a hook, a short story with a value promise (who you are and what viewers get), and a small call to action. Suggests 1.5 to 3 minutes for long form and 30 to 45 seconds for short form. Practical delivery tips: use the script as a framework, practice the hook, and record in short sections.
- **Caveats:** Published by a microphone seller. Written for a channel intro, so adapt it to a cross-platform video and to a world frame.

## 9. Content pillars: different perspectives (days 1 to 3)

"Content pillar" is used in several ways. Some sources define pillars by topic, some by audience, some by purpose or format, and some include the creator's own story. Note also that in SEO, "pillar content" means a comprehensive guide page, which is a different idea.

### Quillbot: Content Pillars, what they are, tips and examples
- **Link:** https://quillbot.com/blog/content-writing/content-pillars/
- **Type:** Article
- **Status:** candidate
- **Role:** practice
- **Layer / day:** Concepts. Days 1 to 3 (proposed)
- **What it offers:** Distinguishes content pillars (main themes), content clusters and content formats, and argues pillars should reflect what the audience wants to learn.
- **Caveats:** Brand and business oriented.

### Buffer: How to Create Content Pillars for Social Media
- **Link:** https://buffer.com/resources/content-pillars-for-social-media/
- **Type:** Article with a template
- **Status:** candidate
- **Role:** practice
- **Layer / day:** Tools. Day 2 or 3 (proposed)
- **What it offers:** Defines a pillar as a key theme you want to be known for, notes that most creators combine several, and gives a pillar ideation template with creator examples.

### Metricool: Content buckets vs content pillars
- **Link:** https://metricool.com/content-buckets
- **Type:** Article
- **Status:** candidate
- **Role:** practice
- **Layer / day:** Tools. Day 2 or 3 (proposed)
- **What it offers:** Buckets organize content by purpose, format or role in the audience journey, while pillars organize by topic. Suggests mapping each post to one pillar and one bucket, as a matrix that generates repeatable ideas.

### Picsart: What are content pillars and how to choose yours
- **Link:** https://picsart.com/blog/choose-your-content-pillars/
- **Type:** Article
- **Status:** candidate
- **Role:** practice
- **Layer / day:** Tools. Day 2 or 3 (proposed)

### SocialPilot: How to Create Social Media Content Pillars

### Captions: Content pillars guide (includes the Vsauce example)

## 11. Choosing your focus (day 1)

Methods for narrowing a list of interests to a few focus topics. Most come from product and project management and are adapted for a personal world.

### Derek Sivers: "Hell yeah or no" (quoted in an AVC post)
- **Link:** https://avc.com/?p=15988
- **Type:** Blog post quoting Sivers
- **Status:** kept
- **Role:** base for the heck yes pass
- **Layer / day:** Tools. Day 1
- **What it offers:** Every opportunity should be a clear yes or a clear no, and what falls in the middle is ignored.
- **Caveats:** Second-hand. Find Sivers' original post.

### Inc.: Warren Buffett's 25/5 rule has been debunked
- **Link:** https://www.inc.com/marcel-schwantes/warren-buffett-25-5-rule-career-goals.html

### ProductLift: ICE prioritization method

## 12. Habits and skipped days (day 0)

### Lally et al. (2010), summarized by the British Psychological Society
- **Link:** https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit
- **Paper:** European Journal of Social Psychology, doi:10.1002/ejsp.674
- **Type:** Research summary
- **Status:** kept
- **Role:** base for the skip rule
- **Layer / day:** System. Day 0
- **What it offers:** Missing a single day had little effect on later habit formation. Repeat the behavior daily if you can, but do not worry excessively if you miss a day or two.

### ScienceBlog: A missed day did not set back habit formation
- **Link:** https://scienceblog.com/missing-a-single-day-didnt-significantly-set-back-the-forming-of-a-new-habit-in-a-ucl-study-which-found-new-daily-habits-took-an-average-of-66-days-to-become-automatic/
- **Type:** News summary
- **Status:** kept
- **Role:** support
- **Layer / day:** System. Day 0
- **What it offers:** Summarizes the Lally study (96 people, 12 weeks) and two more studies. One is the Google field experiment (2,508 employees, Beshears et al., Management Science), where people paid for exercising only in a fixed time window dropped by 10 percentage points after payments stopped, versus 5 points for those with flexible timing. The other is Sharif and Shu on emergency reserves (more than 270 students and staff), where 55% with reserve days met their goal the day after a miss, versus 37% with a strict seven-day target.
- **Caveats:** A summary of other studies. Read the originals before citing numbers formally. The studies are small and come from exercise and step goals.
